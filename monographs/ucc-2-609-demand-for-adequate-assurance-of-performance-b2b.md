## Executive Summary
Under Uniform Commercial Code (UCC) § 2-609, when reasonable grounds for insecurity arise regarding a B2B buyer's payment or a vendor's delivery, a party may suspend performance and demand written Adequate Assurance within 30 days—converting silence into an automatic repudiation and breach of contract.

## Quantitative Reference Table
| UCC Statutory Stage | Governing Code Section | Statutory Deadline | Legal Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Written Demand for Assurance | UCC § 2-609(1) | Immediate upon commercial insecurity | Legally authorizes immediate suspension of shipment/work |
| Failure to Provide Assurance | UCC § 2-609(4) | Exceeding 30 Calendar Days | Constitutes automatic repudiation of the entire contract |
| Seller's Action for Price & Damages | UCC § 2-709 & § 2-710 | Upon repudiation | Full contract price + incidental storage/financing damages |

## Technical Breakdown & Actionable Protocol
Citing UCC § 2-609(4) in a formal certified notice prevents the defaulting counterparty from claiming you breached the agreement when you pause work or hold inventory for non-payment.
